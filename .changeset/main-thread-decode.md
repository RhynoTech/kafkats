Spend less event-loop time decoding and encoding record batches. The native `snappy` library is now called synchronously for blocks of up to 64 KiB decoded (32 KiB to compress), which removes a libuv threadpool round trip per Xerial chunk or one-record batch: on JSON records, consumer main-thread CPU per wire MiB drops from about 15 to 6-7 ms for Java-produced snappy batches and from 34-38 to 6-8 ms for broker-recompressed one-record batches. The built-in gzip codec now uses Node's async zlib above 8 KiB of input or 64 KiB of output, so a 1 MiB gzip producer batch no longer blocks the event loop for about 10 ms. This costs throughput where one gzip partition is decode-bound, since its batches now wait on the threadpool in turn: decoding 120-record gzip batches (about 400 KiB each) offline fell from about 120k to 95-105k records/s and used 5-20% more process CPU, while main-thread CPU per wire MiB halved (24 to 12 ms) and the longest event-loop stall fell from about 35 to 9 ms. A producer yields to the event loop every 2 ms while it encodes the batches of one drain. Reading a fetch response yields to the event loop between partitions every 2 ms, decoding records for a poll or ahead of one yields every 2 ms between records, and decompressing a Xerial-framed snappy batch yields every 256 KiB of output. The pure-JS CRC32C fallback used without `@node-rs/crc32` is about twice as fast, and the client logs an info line recommending `@node-rs/crc32` when it is missing. `gzipCodec.compress()` and `decompress()` now reject on corrupt input instead of throwing synchronously.
